Most micro SaaS projects fail for one reason:

They were built before they were validated.

Validation is not asking your friends if it’s a good idea.

Validation is proving someone will:

- pay
- commit
- or schedule a call

Before you write code.

---

## Day 1: Define the Pain Clearly

Not “an AI tool for marketers.”

Instead:

> A reporting automation tool for small agencies spending 3+ hours per week building client reports.

Specific pain.
Specific user.
Specific outcome.

If you can’t define it in one sentence, it’s too broad.

---

## Day 2: Find 25 Real People

Where they already exist:

- Reddit communities
- LinkedIn searches
- Niche Facebook groups
- Indie communities
- Direct outreach lists

Do not build in isolation.

---

## Day 3–4: Direct Outreach

Message template:

> I’m exploring a small tool that helps [specific user] reduce [specific pain].  
> Would you be open to a 10-minute call to share how you’re handling this today?

You’re not selling.

You’re collecting signal.

---

## Day 5: Pre-Sell or Capture Commitment

Options:

- Landing page with waitlist
- Stripe payment link
- “Early access” discount
- Letter of intent

If no one commits, adjust positioning.

Do not build yet.

---

## Day 6: Refine the Offer

Based on feedback:

- narrow the problem
- simplify the outcome
- reduce features

Micro SaaS wins by being small.

For asset thinking:
→ **[Digital Assets](/digital-assets)**

---

## Day 7: Build the Smallest Useful Version

Not version 1.0.

Version 0.1.

One core feature.
One measurable outcome.

Then iterate based on usage.

## The Real Rule

Validation > Code  
Signal > Ego  
Revenue > Features  

Micro SaaS works when:

- it solves a painful workflow
- it produces a measurable outcome
- it charges for value delivered

Build small.
Validate fast.
Iterate weekly.
